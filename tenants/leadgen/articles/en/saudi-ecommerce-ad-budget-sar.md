Agency guides suggest a Saudi online store can start with around SAR 15,000–30,000 per month spread across Google, Meta and Snapchat, while smaller stores can run a test with a few thousand riyals first. For digital marketing as a whole, one published range for Saudi Arabia runs from SAR 3,000 to more than SAR 60,000 a month. These are agency estimates, not official benchmarks, so treat them as a starting guide and adjust once you have your own results.

Below, we break the budget down by channel, show what clicks, leads and first purchases tend to cost, and explain how to build a plan that suits your store.

## What is a sensible monthly budget for a Saudi online store?

There is no single right figure. The amount that makes sense depends on your margins, your products and how fast you want to learn what works. Published guides offer a useful frame:

- **Overall digital marketing:** 247agency gives a broad range of SAR 3,000 to SAR 60,000+ per month.
- **Multi-channel e-commerce starting point:** thehovi suggests SAR 15,000–30,000 per month across Google, Meta and Snapchat.
- **Small tests:** localcitysolutions puts small Facebook test campaigns at SAR 1,000–3,000 per month. This is an older source, so use it with some caution.

If paid advertising is new to you, a small test on one or two channels lets you gather real data before moving to multi-channel spending.

### Google Ads

digitalwasfa gives a Google Ads spend range of SAR 3,000–20,000 per month. If an agency manages the account for you, nalksolutions notes that fees can be set at 10% to 20% of ad spend. Budget for that fee on top of what you pay the platform itself.

### Meta (Facebook and Instagram)

Altasweeqi lists Meta costs of SAR 0.40–4 per click and SAR 8–90 per lead. A spread that wide shows how much results can shift depending on the product, the audience and the creative.

### TikTok

thehovi recommends at least SAR 5,000 a month to test TikTok, moving up to SAR 15,000–30,000 for steady campaigns. The same source reports Saudi costs of $0.20–$0.80 per click and $8–$25 per lead.

### Snapchat

According to BIMO, there is no official Saudi Snapchat CPM, because neither Snap nor any regulator publishes CPM split by Gulf market. Any figure you come across is an estimate, so your own test results are the most dependable guide.

## What does a lead or first purchase cost?

For an online store, the figure that usually matters most is what it costs to get someone to buy. 23hublab puts cost per first purchase in Saudi Arabia at $6.70–$21.30. Set that against your average order value and margin to judge whether a channel can cover its costs. Lead costs on Meta (SAR 8–90) and TikTok ($8–$25) are helpful reference points if you gather sign-ups or enquiries before a sale.

## Why do costs change during the year?

Busy seasons push prices up. Altasweeqi notes that Meta click costs for e-commerce can go past SAR 3 during Ramadan. If your sales peak then, set aside extra budget and get campaigns ready early instead of scrambling at the last minute.

The market is also getting busier. A Snap and Kearney study found Saudi digital ad spending grew 23.5% year-on-year in 2024, the highest growth rate across MENA. More advertisers can mean more competition for the same audiences.

## How to build your budget step by step

1. **Work out what a customer is worth.** Use your average order value and margin to decide the most you can afford to pay per first purchase.
2. **Choose one or two channels to start.** Focus on where your buyers are most likely to be, rather than spreading a small budget thinly.
3. **Set a test budget.** Use the lower ends of the ranges above, such as SAR 1,000–3,000 for a Facebook test or SAR 5,000 for TikTok.
4. **Allow for management fees.** If an agency runs your ads, factor in a fee; one guide puts Google Ads management at 10–20% of ad spend.
5. **Track cost per lead and per purchase.** Compare your figures with the published ranges.
6. **Grow what works.** Move towards the SAR 15,000–30,000 multi-channel level only when your results support it.
7. **Prepare for peak seasons.** Hold back extra budget for Ramadan and other busy periods.

Every figure here comes from agency guides or industry reports, not official statistics. Sources measure costs differently, and some quote US dollars rather than riyals. Use these ranges to set expectations, then let your own campaign data guide where you spend.
